I’ve put together a full plan for adding online ordering to Vesopa — takeaway and delivery apps for Android and iOS, plus QR ordering at the table — with every order landing straight on the till. It’s attached as a PDF, and it’s built to deliver all three channels within two months.
The short version: we already own the hard part. We run the till, the menu, the pricing rules, the floor plan and the card payments. What we don’t own is the moment the customer decides what to eat — and that moment currently costs our venues 14–30% commission to Deliveroo, Uber Eats and Just Eat. On £10,000 of monthly online orders that’s £1,400–£3,000 a month leaving the business, every month, often on customers the venue introduced itself.
What I’m proposing to build:
• A consumer app for Android and iOS — find a venue, order for collection or delivery, pay with Apple Pay or Google Pay, track the order. This is the main focus of the document.
• QR ordering at the table — scan the code on the table, the menu opens in the phone browser with no app to install, order and either pay there or pay at the counter.
• A new Orders tab on the till where takeaway, delivery and table orders arrive live, are accepted or rejected by a clerk, and print to the kitchen through the routing we already have.
• Back-office controls so a venue can set its menu per channel, generate and print a QR code for each table, and point its own website or domain at its ordering page.
The eight weeks:
Weeks 1–2 Foundations — data model, server-side pricing, live order push, the till Orders tab
Weeks 2–4 QR table ordering — first venue taking real orders at week 4
Weeks 3–7 The Android and iOS apps — submitted to both stores at week 7
Weeks 5–7 Delivery — zones, fees, driver dispatch
Weeks 7–8 Venue domains and branding, trial support, store-review buffer
I want to be straight with you about one thing: two months works because the app track and the web track overlap from week 3 rather than running end to end. Done sequentially by one person, the same scope is closer to twenty weeks. That overlap is the assumption the date rests on, and it’s the fourth item below.
Four things I need decided in week 1
1. App Store publishing model. One Vesopa app containing every venue, or a branded app per venue? This matters more than it sounds — Apple’s guideline 4.2.6 rejects near-identical template apps submitted from one developer account, and a rejection can put the whole account at risk. My recommendation is one Vesopa app with a venue picker, which is the model Apple explicitly names as acceptable, with per-venue branded apps sold later as an add-on published from the venue’s own account.
2. Do we get into delivery logistics? My recommendation is no — support venues who already have their own drivers, and plug in a third-party courier later. Building driver supply is a different business, and on a two-month run it would sink the date.
3. The trial venue — confirmed by week 3, because the week-4 go-live depends on it. I need one cooperative venue that will take real orders, tell us when it breaks, and let us sit in during service. Ideally one with an existing aggregator bill, so we can measure what we actually saved them.
4. How the two parallel tracks are resourced from week 3 onwards. If it stays one developer, the honest move is to hold the two-month date for QR ordering and the apps and let delivery follow, rather than ship all three half-finished.
There are also two things that need to start in week 1 — neither needs a line of code from me, and both are on the critical path. First, opening the Apple Developer organisation account: the D-U-N-S verification is regularly a three-week wait, which is nearly half this schedule and the likeliest single reason we would miss the date. Second, confirming with Dojo / Paymentsense what their online, card-not-present product supports — our integration today is card-present only, and every channel depends on taking money online. If their offering is thin, I need to know in week 1, not week 5.
The PDF covers the architecture, the app screen by screen, payments and store submission, what changes on the till, the data-model work, and an honest list of what will go wrong and how we handle it — including a few things worth knowing early, such as eat-in versus takeaway VAT needing different rates on the same product.
